Why these three?

A funder that has never seen your contracts prices wide, because it is guessing at a dispute rate it cannot measure. Standing behind it for the first slice of any dispute is what buys the tighter rate — that is how the first deal gets done. Once there is a track record, the rate falls again.

What these words actually mean
Escrow
Money held by neither side. Your customer can't spend it and you can't touch it until you've delivered.
Repaid first
When the escrow releases, the funder is paid before you are, up to its cap. That priority is the thing it is actually buying.
Your tail
Whatever the escrow releases above the funder's cap. It's yours, but you're paid last.
First loss
If there's a refund, it comes out of your tail before it touches the funder. That's the risk you keep — and the reason the rate is lower than borrowing.
Is this a loan?

No. Nobody lends you anything and you don't owe anyone a repayment. You are selling a claim on money that is already sitting in escrow.

So there is nothing to secure against your house, no personal guarantee to sign, and nothing to claw back if the deal later sours.

What happens if there's a dispute?

Your tail absorbs the first — of any refund. The funder's charge covers another —, because it advanced less than its cap. Past — the funder is out of pocket — which is why a refund percentage is set by a registered arbitrator, not by either side.
