StableDrop — How a payment works

A buyer's money is held safely until it's time to pay the seller — for goods or services (say, a builder finishing a job). Money only ever moves on terms the buyer and seller signed with their own wallets — StableDrop has no power to move it. Here's every step.

Seller creates a payment request

Seller · in their browser

The seller — whether selling an item or providing a service (like a builder quoting for a job) — fills in an amount, a description, a release date, and the buyer's email. This just creates a record in our system — nothing happens with any money yet, and nothing is on the blockchain.

Buyer gets a payment link

Buyer · by email

We email the buyer a link to the request (or the seller can share the link directly). Still no money and no blockchain involved — just a notification.

Off-chain Backend (email)
3

Buyer opens the link & signs in

Buyer · in their browser

The buyer opens the link and connects their own crypto wallet. Connecting is not signing — it just lets the app show the buyer what they're being asked to approve. They review the amount and the release date before signing anything.

Off-chain Frontend
4

Buyer accepts → a secure escrow "vault" is created on the blockchain

Buyer accepts · vault created on-chain (we cover the blockchain fee)

When the buyer accepts, a brand-new escrow contract — think of it as a digital safe — is created on the public blockchain. Setting up the empty safe costs nothing and moves no money, so we handle and pay for that step. It's programmed to only ever release money to the buyer or the seller. No money moves into it yet — and no money ever can, until the buyer signs it across with their own wallet in step 5.

On-chain Non-custodial vault No funds move without the buyer's signature
5

Buyer approves & deposits the funds

Buyer · signs with their own wallet

The buyer approves the payment in their wallet and the funds move directly from the buyer's wallet into the escrow vault. The buyer signs this transaction themselves, in their own wallet — their private key never leaves their device, StableDrop never sees it, and the money never touches a StableDrop wallet. Without that signature, the funds simply don't move.

On-chain Signed by the buyer's wallet Signed in browser Non-custodial — we never hold it
6

Both parties are notified — money is now locked & protected

Buyer & Seller · by email

We email both sides to confirm the funds are safely locked in the vault until the release date. The buyer is protected; the seller knows the money is committed.

Off-chain Backend (email)
7

On the release date, the seller is paid automatically

Seller · paid directly on-chain (we run the release trigger for you)

Once the release date passes with no dispute, the funds go straight from the vault to the seller's wallet. At that point the outcome is already settled, so anyone at all can call the contract's release function on the blockchain to set the payout in motion — and whoever calls it, the contract will only ever pay the seller's address, and only once the release date and time has actually passed. Both of those — the recipient address and the exact maturity date and time — are written into the contract when the buyer signs in step 5. The maturity date and time can never change. The recipient address can only ever be changed by the current recipient themselves, signing with their own wallet — nobody else can redirect the payout. StableDrop runs an automated trigger for every contract that reaches maturity, purely as a convenience. Triggering is not control: we can't change who gets paid, how much, or release it a moment earlier than the date the buyer agreed to — the blockchain enforces all of it.

What if there's a problem?

Steps 1–7 are the smooth path. If the buyer isn't happy before the release date, they can open a dispute instead — and even then, the money still only ever moves on signatures from the buyer's, seller's and arbiter's own wallets. StableDrop has no power to move the funds.

A

Buyer opens a dispute

Buyer only · before the release date

If something's wrong (an item not received or not as described, or work that wasn't finished or done properly), the buyer can raise a dispute — but only while the money is still locked and only before the release date. Once that date passes with no dispute, the seller is simply paid as normal. The seller can't open a dispute; they just receive payment on time.

On-chain Raised from the buyer's own wallet Buyer-initiated
B

The money freezes in the vault

Funds stay locked on-chain

The moment a dispute is opened, the funds are frozen inside the escrow vault. The seller can no longer claim, and the money still doesn't move to anyone — it just waits until the dispute is settled. As always, it never goes to a StableDrop wallet, and StableDrop has no power to move it.

On-chain Non-custodial — still locked in the vault StableDrop can't move the funds
C

The split is decided by a 2-of-3 vote

Buyer · Seller · Arbiter (a neutral tie-breaker)

Each contract has three voters: the buyer, the seller, and a neutral arbiter who can only help decide — never take the funds. Each vote is signed by that party's own wallet, so nobody can vote on anyone else's behalf. When any two of the three agree on how to split the money (e.g. full refund, full payment, or anything in between like 60/40), that decision is locked in. Until two signatures match, the money doesn't move at all.

On-chain Each vote signed by its own wallet 2-of-3 agreement
D

Funds are released — only ever to buyer and seller

Buyer and/or Seller · paid directly on-chain

Once two matching signed votes are in, the agreed split is paid out straight from the vault to the buyer and/or the seller. That payout happens only because of signatures from the parties' own wallets — StableDrop has no power to move the funds and no signature that could. The blockchain physically prevents the money from going anywhere else — not to the arbiter, not to StableDrop. The two shares always add up to 100% of the escrow.

On-chain Released by the parties' signatures Non-custodial payout
Non-custodial, end to end — your money moves only on your signature. StableDrop never holds your money and has no power to move it. Money gets into the vault only when the buyer signs the transfer with their own wallet, on their own device, with a private key we never see. Money gets out only on the terms locked in by that signature — to the seller after the release date, or split between buyer and seller by a 2-of-3 vote signed by their own wallets. Once a contract has matured, anyone can run the release code on the blockchain, and it makes no difference who does: the recipient addresses and the exact maturity date and time are written into the contract. We can't change any of it — not the who, not the how much, not the when. The only address change the contract allows is a recipient re-pointing their own payout, signed with the wallet that was already due to be paid. Funds go buyer → vault → seller (or back to the buyer if a dispute is resolved that way), all on the blockchain, controlled only by code and by your signatures. The arbiter and StableDrop can help decide a dispute, but the contract makes it impossible for them to take the funds. We just handle the easy parts — the request, the notifications, setting up the empty vault, running the release trigger, and covering the blockchain fees so you don't have to.
